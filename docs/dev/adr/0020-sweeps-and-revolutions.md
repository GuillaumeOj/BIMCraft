# 0020. Sweeps and revolutions

- Status: Accepted
- Requirements: GEO-02, GEO-03, GEO-09 (later), ENG-04
- Issues: sweep and revolution [#74](https://github.com/GuillaumeOj/BIMCraft/issues/74), epic [#46](https://github.com/GuillaumeOj/BIMCraft/issues/46)

## Context

GEO-02 asks for solids by extrusion, by sweep along a path and by revolution. Extrusion exists (`Solid::extrude`, [ADR 0008](0008-arc-faceting.md)). A sweep moves a `Profile` along a 3D path of lines and circular arcs (curved beams, handrails, wall sweeps); a revolution turns it around an axis of its plane (round bases, domes, rings, turned members).

The B-rep has planar faces only (GEO-03); true curved faces are GEO-09, Later. So both constructors facet what they trace, as extrusion facets arcs. Two new questions arise: how finely to facet a curve that no input arc describes (the circle a point travels on), and what a solid must avoid so that `Solid::from_faces`, which checks each face and edge but not that faces avoid each other, never accepts a solid that crosses itself.

## Decision

This record covers both constructors, `Solid::revolve` and `Solid::sweep`, so that they share their rules.

### D1. Planar faces, faceted curves

- **Profile arcs** become chords with `Profile::faceted()`, within `ARC_CHORD_TOLERANCE` (1 mm), as for extrusion.
- **The curve traced by the motion** (the circle of a revolution, an arc of a sweep path) is also cut into steps within 1 mm, with the **largest radius any profile point travels on**: `r_max` for a revolution (the farthest profile vertex from the axis), `R + d` for a path arc of radius `R` with the profile reaching `d` from the path. A point nearer the axis travels on a smaller circle, so its deviation is smaller still: no point of the solid is more than 1 mm from the shape the faceted profile sweeps.
- **A revolution steps by chords; a sweep by a tangent polygon.** A revolution's sections lie on chords of each circle (inside it). A path arc becomes its **tangent (circumscribed) polygon**: for n steps of α = θ/n, n − 1 sides touching the arc at angles α, 2α, …, plus a half side at each end, along the arc's exact end tangents, that is start → V₀ → … → V_{n−1} → end with V_k at angle (k + ½)α and radius R / cos(α/2). Three reasons. The end pieces follow the true tangents, so the start face is normal to the true start tangent and a line meeting the arc tangentially makes no corner. Every joint inside the arc is a radial plane of the arc, so the self-intersection rules below reduce to exact radial conditions. And the volume has a closed form. A tangent polygon of radius r stays within s of its circle exactly when the chords of a circle of radius r + s do (1/cos h − 1 ≤ s/r ⇔ 1 − cos h ≤ s/(r + s)), so a sweep calls the same helper at radius **R + d + s**. Its points are computed from the arc's start, V_k − start = R / cos h · (−2 sin((k + 1)h) sin(kh) · outward + sin((2k + 1)h) · tangent) with h = α/2, so a large radius does not swamp a short arc, and the last point is the arc's end, bit for bit.
- **One formula.** ADR 0008's `n = ⌈ |θ| / (2 acos(1 − s / r)) ⌉` is a single helper, `step_count(radius, sweep, max_step, minimum)`, used by profile faceting (no step limit), by revolution and by sweep. It uses `libm` (ADR 0003), computes acos(1 − s/r) as 2 asin(√(s / 2r)), equal in exact arithmetic, so that a huge radius (a nearly straight path arc) does not round 1 − s/r to 1 and the count to infinity, and returns a whole-valued `f64`, so callers compare it with their budget before converting it.
- **Steps are at most 90°** (`max_step = π/2`), so a full turn has at least 4 steps, however small the radius: below half the tolerance a single chord could otherwise span half a turn, which gives faces that flip over. A sweep along a path arc takes at least one step.
- **Side faces are planar by construction.** Between two sections of a revolution, a profile edge sweeps a trapezoid (its two section edges are parallel, both perpendicular to the axis); an edge with a vertex on the axis sweeps a triangle. Along a straight piece of a sweep, each profile vertex runs parallel to the piece, so a profile edge sweeps a trapezoid whose two long edges are parallel.
- **Face budget.** A solid has at most `MAX_SOLID_FACES = 65 536` faces (16 × `MAX_PROFILE_EDGES`, 4096), shared by both constructors. The count is computed from the step count and the number of profile edges *before* any face is built, so hostile input cannot allocate millions of them; exceeding it is a typed error (`TooManyFaces`). The bound keeps the memory (a few megabytes) and the validation of one solid, which is linear in its faces, small and predictable, and it admits every ordinary element: a unit sphere takes 36 × 71 = 2 556 faces, a ring of 20 m radius with a rectangular section 4 × 280 = 1 120. At a radius of 1 m (71 steps) a full turn admits a profile of up to 922 edges; at 14 km from the axis, near the limit of the model extent (about 8 300 steps), one of up to 7. The limit is a safety net, not a design capacity, and raising it later only admits more inputs.

### D2. Revolution (`Solid::revolve`)

- **Input**: the `Profile` in the plane z = 0, an axis in that plane (`RevolutionAxis`: a 2D point and a non-zero `Vector2` direction), and an angle in radians, 0 < angle ≤ 2π. A positive angle turns by the right-hand rule around the axis direction. The result is in model coordinates; the start section is the input profile bit for bit.
- **The profile lies on one side of the axis.** A vertex within the tolerance of the axis (`WITHIN_TOLERANCE`, ADR 0001) is **on** the axis: it is a single vertex of the solid, not one per step, and a profile edge between two vertices on the axis gives no face. An edge with one end on the axis gives triangles (cones), with both ends off it quadrilaterals. A profile with vertices on both sides is rejected (`ProfileCrossesAxis`).
- **Partial turn**: two end faces (the profile at the start and end angles) and one shell; each profile hole is a tunnel from end face to end face, so genus = number of holes.
- **Full turn** (angle exactly 2π): no end faces, the last section is the first. A profile away from the axis gives a ring (genus 1); one that touches it, a solid of genus 0; each profile hole gives a closed ring-shaped cavity, a void shell of genus 1 with negative volume. A profile that touches the axis in two separate places (a C shape) also encloses what lies between them: one more void shell, of genus 0.
- **Pinched axis vertex.** In a full turn, a vertex on the axis whose two profile edges both leave the axis would be the tip of two cones: the faces around it form two circles, not one fan, and the solid touches itself at a point. It is rejected (`PinchedOnAxis`). A vertex next to an edge along the axis is fine. In a partial turn the two end faces join the circles into one fan, so the same profile is accepted. In a full turn, a vertex between two edges along the axis lies in no face and is left out of the solid.
- **No almost closed solid.** An angle past a half turn whose end faces come within the tolerance of each other at `r_max` is rejected (`NearlyFullTurn`); an angle of exactly 2π is the full turn.
- **Volume**: Pappus for the exact shape, V = A · θ · r̄ (r̄: distance of the profile's centroid to the axis). For n steps of α = θ/n the faceted solid has V = n · sin α · A_f · r̄_f, with A_f and r̄_f those of the faceted profile: each step is a trapezoid prism, whose volume is sin α times the profile's first moment about the axis. Tests check the faceted formula to rounding and Pappus within the bound below.

### D3. Sweep (`Solid::sweep`)

- **Path**: a public `Path` type, validated at construction like `Profile`: a chain of at most `MAX_PATH_SEGMENTS = 256` 3D segments, each ending exactly where the next begins, either `Line { start, end }` or `Arc { start, through, end }` (three distinct non-collinear points fix the plane, radius and sense; full circles cannot be given this way). Validation rejects (`PathError`, segments by index): an empty path, too many segments, non-finite or out-of-extent coordinates (subnormals included), gaps, lines no longer than the tolerance, degenerate arcs (two points within the tolerance of each other, or the middle point within it of the line through the ends) and **closed paths** (last end within the tolerance of the first start). The arc's centre is computed relative to its start, c = (|u|² (v × w) + |v|² (w × u)) / (2 |w|²) with u = through − start, v = end − start, w = u × v.
- **Placement**: the profile's origin is at the path's start, in the plane normal to the start tangent `t`. A caller-given `reference` vector gives the profile's y axis (its part normal to `t`), and the x axis is y × t, so (x, y, t) is right-handed. A `reference` that is zero or not finite is rejected; so is one parallel to `t`. ADR 0001 has no angular tolerance, so "parallel" is a distance: the reference's unit part normal to `t`, times the profile's reach `d`, is within the tolerance, so it would turn no profile point by more than the tolerance.
- **Frame: rotation-minimizing (parallel transport), by projection.** Along a line the frame does not turn; along an arc it turns with the tangent around the arc's normal; at a corner by the smallest rotation taking the incoming direction to the outgoing one. A beam in a vertical plane keeps its profile upright. No rotation is computed: each section is the previous one moved along its piece onto the next joint plane, S_{k+1} = S_k + λ t_k with λ = ((P_{k+1} − S_k) · m) / (t_k · m), where m = t_k + t_{k+1} is the normal of the plane bisecting the corner (normal to t at the path's end). Projecting along the incoming direction onto the bisecting plane gives exactly the profile turned by the minimal rotation: a profile offset w along the turn's axis is unchanged by both, and one in the plane of the turn, b · (k × t₁), lands on b · (−tan(φ/2), 1) in the frame (t₁, k × t₁) either way. So the start section is placed once, every later one follows, and the side faces are planar by construction, their long edges all along their piece. Rounding accumulates from section to section (up to `MAX_SWEEP_PIECES`); a test on a 747-piece arc checks that the end face stays within 1 nm of the plane normal to the end tangent and keeps its orientation. A fixed-reference frame (`IfcFixedReferenceSweptAreaSolid`) is out of scope.
- **Corners** (tangent discontinuities between segments, and the joints of an arc's tangent polygon) are **mitre joints**: the section there is the profile projected onto the plane that bisects the corner. A turn back of 180° is rejected (`DoublesBack`) when the two directions' sum, times `d`, is within the tolerance; sharper turns that are not quite 180° are left to the mitre check below, which they fail.
- **Pieces**: a line is one straight piece, an arc of n steps n + 1. At most `MAX_SWEEP_PIECES = 1024` pieces (`TooManyPieces`), and pieces × profile edges + 2 at most `MAX_SOLID_FACES` (`TooManyFaces`), both counted before anything is built. 1024 pieces admit a full turn of 100 m radius, and keep the pairwise test below to half a million pairs.
- **Ends**: two end faces; one shell; genus = number of profile holes.
- **No self-intersection**, which `Solid::from_faces` does not check. The checks run in this order, all exact on the faceted profile's vertices:
  1. *Arc too tight* (`ArcTooTight`): on a path arc of radius R, the profile point nearest the arc's axis, at `inner` = R − (its offset towards the axis), runs `inner` · tan(α/2) along each half piece at the arc's ends; that must exceed the tolerance. With α/2 ≤ 45° the point then also stays more than the tolerance from the axis, so the inner side cannot fold, and a profile reaching nearly to the axis is refused as too tight rather than as crossing mitres. Parallel transport keeps that offset the same in every section of the arc, so the section where the arc starts decides.
  2. *Mitres crossing* (`MitresCross`): along every straight piece, each profile vertex runs a length λ greater than the tolerance between the piece's two end planes. λ is the length the projection above computes anyway.
  3. *Distant pieces* (`SelfIntersecting`), for every pair of pieces that are not neighbours. Pieces of **one arc bounded by radial planes at both ends** are exempt: given checks 1 and 2, each lies in its own wedge of the arc between two radial half-planes, and the wedges are disjoint because θ < 2π. A piece is so bounded inside the arc, at an end of the path, or where the arc meets another segment without turning (the joint's plane is the radial plane within the tolerance across the profile). An arc's first or last piece cut by a corner's mitre is not: its mitre's tip can reach back into the wedges at the arc's far end (a test sweeps a line into a 358.9° arc and finds that pair). When both ends of an arc are radial and it turns more than half a turn, its two end faces must also be more than the tolerance apart at the profile point nearest the arc's axis: 2 (R − inward) sin((2π − θ)/2). Every other pair must pass one of two sound tests:
     - *Reach*: each piece lies within ρ = d / cos(φ/2) of its axis segment, φ the turn at either end (φ = 0 at the path's ends). A mitre's tip lies at d / cos(φ/2) from the corner, so a bare 2 · d, the first draft's test, is not sound at corners. Axis segments farther apart than ρ₁ + ρ₂ + the tolerance cannot meet.
     - *Separating plane*: each piece lies in the convex hull of its two sections. If every vertex of one piece lies more than the tolerance beyond every vertex of the other, measured along the normal of a plane, the plane separates them. The planes tried are the joint where the first piece's segment ends and the one where the second piece's segment starts. Without this test every fillet (line, arc, line) and every S-curve would be refused: a line and the second piece of the arc after it are always within 2 · d.

  The test is **conservative**: it may refuse a legal sweep whose distant parts come close without touching, for instance a U-turn whose middle leg is shorter than about 2.8 · d, or a line meeting a long arc at a sharp corner. It is sound, simple, and bounded: the reach test is O(pieces²), at most about 5 · 10⁵ pairs; a pair that falls back to the separating planes reads both pieces' 2 × (profile vertices) section points for each of two planes, so the worst case, 1024 pieces of a 64-edge profile all falling back, is about 5 · 10⁵ pairs × 2 planes × 2 pieces × 128 points ≈ 3 · 10⁸ dot products, under a second in a release build, which is acceptable for the MVP. Each piece's interval along each junction plane could be computed once (at most 257 planes) to make a pair O(1); that is left until a real path needs it. No benchmark is added: the worst case is synthetic, and a regression would show in the property tests' run time. A tighter exact test needs face-pair intersection and is left to a later decision.
- **Volume**: for straight pieces with mitres, V = A_f × the length of the polyline traced by the profile's centroid (the length between a piece's two end planes is linear in the profile point, so its average over the area is the centroid's). A straight path normal to the profile gives `Solid::extrude`'s solid, face for face. Along a path arc the centroid traces the tangent polygon of radius r̄ (its distance from the arc's axis), 2n half sides of r̄ tan(α/2): V = 2n · tan(α/2) · A_f · r̄. That is slightly **more** than Pappus' θ · A_f · r̄, the tangent polygon lying outside the circle; tests check both, the excess within the bound below.
- **Closed paths** (a sweep around a loop) are rejected: a ring in one plane is a full revolution, and parallel transport does not close up the frame after a loop on a 3D path.

### D4. Errors and security

New `thiserror` enums: `RevolutionError` (here), and for the sweep `PathError` and `SweepError`; each constructor's error wraps `ProfileError` (faceting) and `BrepError` (the final check) like `ExtrusionError`. `SweepError` does not wrap `PathError`: `Solid::sweep` takes a `Path` that is already valid, so such a variant could never be returned. No variant carries coordinates (SEC-03); loops and segments are named by index. Every input is hostile: NaN, infinities, subnormals, out-of-extent values, zero vectors, huge counts. No input may panic.

### D5. Determinism and placement

Trigonometry through `libm` only (ADR 0003): no platform maths, no `std::time`, no allocation proportional to unbounded input (ENG-04, ARCH-01/02). The constructors live in `crates/geom/src/brep/` (`revolve.rs`, `sweep.rs`) and the `Path` type in `crates/geom/src/path/`. Golden values (`tests/golden.txt`) include revolutions and sweeps, and run natively and under WebAssembly.

### The approximation bound

The faceted profile is within 1 mm of the exact profile, and the sweep of the faceted profile with chorded (revolution) or tangent (sweep) steps is within 1 mm of the exact sweep of the faceted profile. The solid is therefore within **about 2 mm** of the exact shape, worst case where both extremes meet (a profile arc point farthest from the axis at the middle of a step). Volumes follow: a revolution lies inside the exact solid, and a sweep's path arcs lie partly outside it, each by at most `ARC_CHORD_TOLERANCE` from the steps plus `ARC_CHORD_TOLERANCE` from the profile's chords, times the area of the exact curved surface it replaces (a tube of radii 1 and 2 m, 1 m high: 6π m² × 2 mm ≈ 38 L out of 9.42 m³; tests use this bound). The tests assert it.

2 mm is acceptable because it is well below what a model at the level of detail of building elements carries, and because the alternative costs: face count grows as 1/√tolerance, so 0.5 mm for each source would raise the face count of every round element by 1.4 and slow the booleans for a gap nobody sees. Changing `ARC_CHORD_TOLERANCE` changes stored results (ADR 0008) and the golden file.

## Consequences

- Revolved and swept solids are faceted solids: their volume is the faceted volume. Quantity take-off of such elements should use Pappus' exact value from the section's area and centroid (as for extrusion).
- A caller needing a closed sweep builds a revolution, or two sweeps.
- A profile of more than a few edges at the farthest radii of the model extent cannot be fully revolved: see the face budget.
- True cylindrical, conical and toroidal faces arrive with GEO-09 and a record that supersedes this one for the cases it covers.
- The sweep's global self-intersection test may refuse legal solids; rejection is a typed error, never a repair.
- A swept curved member is a little larger than the exact one (tangent polygon), a revolved one a little smaller (chords); both stay within the approximation bound above.

## Alternatives considered

- **Reject partial support until GEO-09.** Round bases, domes and curved rails could not be modelled before the curved-surface work.
- **Steps from the profile's centroid radius** (or from the path's radius alone). A profile point farther out then travels on a circle whose chords exceed the tolerance, by an amount that grows with the profile's size: a 1 m wide section on a 2 m radius arc would be off by centimetres.
- **A fixed number of steps per turn.** Simple, but the error grows with the radius (ADR 0008).
- **Steps without the 90° limit.** For radii below half the tolerance one chord would span a half turn.
- **Merging coincident axis vertices after building** one copy per step. It would build `n` degenerate faces per axis edge and rely on the merge pass of ADR 0001 to remove them; building the right topology directly is simpler and exact.
- **Accepting a profile that crosses the axis** by splitting it. A cut at the axis is a boolean-style operation with its own tolerance questions, and building elements do not need it.
- **Auto-mirroring a profile on the right of the axis.** Rejected: the right-hand rule is stated for the axis direction, and the profile at angle 0 stays the input profile.
- **An angle above 2π**, or a negative angle. A negative angle is the same as the reversed axis; more than a full turn would overlap itself.
- **Closed sweeps** and **fixed-reference frames**: out of scope for GEO-02, later issues.
- **Exact global self-intersection tests for sweeps.** Needs face-pair intersection and a policy for touching faces; the conservative distance test is sound and cheap.
- **Chords for path arcs, as for revolutions.** The first and last chords would leave the arc at half a step from its tangents, so the start face would not be normal to the path, a tangent line → arc joint would become a corner, and the inner-fold rule would no longer match the mitre rule exactly.
- **A bare 2 · d distance between distant pieces** (the first draft). Unsound at corners, where a mitre's tip reaches d / cos(φ/2) from the path, and, on its own, so strict that it refuses every fillet.
- **An explicit rotation per joint** (Rodrigues). Equivalent to the projection onto the bisecting plane, but it needs a re-orthonormalisation and makes the side faces planar only up to the rotation's rounding.
